# Coordinated multiself (shelved concept)

## Status

Removed from the active cast during Snowflake Step 3. Preserved here as an idea
bank only.

The concept did not earn its additional neuroscience premise. Ruth already
shows damaged overlap among identity bundles, Tess shows resistance to smooth
replacement, and the residual entities show subdivided alternate selves. The
multiself's partial-capture behavior remained an illustration rather than an
indispensable decision, conflict, relationship, reveal, recurring image, or
ending function.

## Core idea

One body contains approximately four semi-autonomous cognitive coalitions that coordinate more like an octopus than like a conventional unitary self. They are not theatrical personalities taking turns. They have overlapping but nonidentical sensory access, memories, preferences, language influence, and motor control.

## Relationship to the projector

The projector can capture the coalitions unevenly:

- one hand begins carrying out Evan's intentions before the verbal self agrees;
- speech sincerely rationalizes Evan's plan;
- another coalition writes warnings, hides components, or sabotages an experiment;
- another preserves memories the captured systems have lost.

Their rarity comes from identity as a negotiated coalition. Selecting one compliant subsystem does not smoothly replace the whole person because the others detect and compensate.

Possible line:

> Part of me believes you. The rest of us think that part has been compromised.

## Dramatic value

- Makes the bundle-of-histories theory visible at human scale.
- Demonstrates that internal disagreement can be protective rather than defective.
- Provides a strange resistance case distinct from Tess's causal rarity.
- Allows partial social capture without requiring the whole person to become a follower.

## Risks

- Could become a gimmick or inaccurate split-brain superpower.
- Adds another large neuroscience premise to an already dense book.
- Must be written as a person with an ordinary life, not an octopus metaphor in shoes.

## Open questions

These questions are inactive unless the concept is deliberately revived.

- Cause: congenital organization, surgery, injury, engineered neural interface, or unknown.
- Degree of shared language and memory.
- Pronouns and self-description.
- Plot role and relationship to Evan, Ruth, Tess, and Lena.
