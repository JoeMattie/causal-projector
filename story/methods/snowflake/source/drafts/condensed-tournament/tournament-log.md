## Drift across generations

- Round 1 produced the only large creative divergence. The winning version
  planted eight-year-old Tess surviving the flooded hull by sound alone
  (motivating her spatio-acoustic contact and terminal geometry), gave Ruth's
  granddaughter a name from the never-used shortlist, made the mass-speech
  sentence "Love you, I'll be back," and named the terminal two-blind-choices
  design as Evan's motor-estimator fix at final scale. Both judges called
  these connective-tissue payoffs decisive.
- Round 2 split the judges (the only tiebreak): a disciplined line-edit pass
  narrowly beat a second developmental pass.
- Rounds 3 and 4 converged to roughly a dozen line edits per candidate;
  judges explicitly noted near-identical texts. Returns diminished sharply
  after round 1.
- Word count drifted 7,308 -> 7,302. Zero dash violations in any surviving
  version. All locked lines and the cat-viewpoint ending survived all four
  generations.
