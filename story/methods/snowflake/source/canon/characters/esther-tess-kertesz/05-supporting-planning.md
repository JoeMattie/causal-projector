## Narrative distance

- Tess's treatment uses evolving close third in the present tense.
- The narration begins near her disciplined, observational habits and can grow
  more intimate as her professional audit becomes a place in Evan, Ruth, and
  Mouse's shared life.
- Tess notices chains: assumptions, missing links, local decisions, and the

- Her spatio-acoustic attention remains physical and specific. Voices,

  than abstract descriptions of relation.

## Organizing spine

The emotional spine is **sole survivor versus shared ending**. Tess has built
an adult life after becoming the single living remainder of a catastrophe
that took her whole immediate family. The final operation confronts her with
the possibility that she may once again survive everyone beside her. Her last
choice matters because she makes it with that danger intact.

The working voice is **verification versus trust**. Tess follows chains,
checks assumptions, and independently verifies claims because those habits
are intelligent, professionally useful, and often morally necessary. They are
not a pathology the love story cures. They are how she recognizes Evan's
result as real, how she discovers the consequences he would rather formalize,
and how she understands the possibility that her own choices helped the garage
lineage become an independent carrier. Evan's secrecy denied her the chance to
withdraw while withdrawal might still have changed her part in that history.

At the end, verification has not failed as a way of knowing. The final choice
is simply beyond it: neither Tess nor Evan can observe the other's answer
before giving their own. Tess does not learn to trust blindly. She knowingly
chooses a shared ending when proof is impossible, with refusal still available
and with the real possibility that she will be left as the sole remainder
again.

The ferry disaster supplies the deepest pressure under this arc, but it does
not explain every choice Tess makes. Her work, skepticism, friendships,
affection, fear, and love remain features of an adult life rather than symptoms
of one childhood event.

Her final choice creates meaning forward. It does not make the ferry disaster,
her family's deaths, Ruth's death, or any erased life necessary in retrospect.

## Accepted opening

The treatment opens in the hospital after the ferry disaster. Eight-year-old
Tess wakes among adults who begin with the fact that she survived and avoid
the fact that nobody else in her immediate family did.

This places the emotional consequence before the accident mechanics. The
opening can carry sensory remnants, reported facts, and the parts of the event
Tess tries to recover, but it does not replay the whole wreck as spectacle or
depend on technical details that remain open.

The adults' impulse to call her survival a miracle introduces the first meaning
other people try to place on her. Tess is still a child. She does not arrive at
her adult philosophy in the hospital, speak like a catastrophe modeler, or
understand the full chain.

When the adults tell her that nobody else survived, Tess asks about each member
of her family separately. She makes them answer separately too. This is a
child's need to know where her people are, not an early speech about evidence
or meaning. It also prevents the dead from entering her story as one collective
absence.

Tess does not answer the word `miracle` in the hospital. It sours through years
of repetition as adults, reporters, doctors, and later acquaintances use her
survival to finish a satisfying story that has no place for the people who
died. Her adult rejection of assigned meaning grows from that history. It is
not a polished conclusion she reaches at eight.

The person-by-person questions are an early expression of her need for exact
answers. They do not make the ferry disaster the sole cause of her professional
discipline or adult character.

## From the ferry to her work

As Tess grows older, she gets the official accident report, rescue records,
and the medical account of her recovery. She studies them closely enough to
understand that her survival came from a long chain of ordinary conditions,
each of which could have broken another way. The records give her causes,
times, decisions, and gaps. They do not give her survival a purpose.

The ferry is the first failed system Tess studies at length, and it leaves her
quick to notice what a clean public account has omitted. Her career still grows
from a wider curiosity. Weather, infrastructure, human judgment, coastlines,
plants, animals, and the ways small failures meet all interest her on their own.
Catastrophe modeling fits the way she thinks and lets her make something useful
without turning her family into the reason she was meant to do it.

The exact age at which Tess receives the records, what remains redacted or
missing, and the particular education and early work that lead her to compound
risk modeling remain open.

## Entry into the garage

Lena first raises Evan's coincidence chains during pub trivia. Tess agrees that
the account is strange, but she will not form a professional opinion from
Lena's summary or agree to meet Evan on friendship alone. She asks for the raw
logs, the precommitted protocol, the complete run history, and the ordinary
instrument records that should expose a mundane dependency.

The material does not convince Tess that Evan has found a way to force an
improbable outcome. It survives enough of her first checks that dismissing it
would be as careless as accepting it. Tess chooses to visit the garage because
the chain has earned a real audit. The favor to Lena has become Tess's own
professional interest before she meets Evan.

Once she has the complete record, Tess explains the longer coincidence chain
that brought her there. A hidden dependency and retrospective choices account
for the whole thing. This does not undo Evan and Lena's earlier precommitted
physical result. It removes the supposed evidence that the effect has already
spread beyond the apparatus.

Evan is skeptical at first. Tess walks him through the dependency, and he
checks her explanation against the original records until he can no longer find
a way around it. Then he is elated. He would rather have a real explanation
that destroys the claim than preserve an exciting mistake, and the correction
gives them a cleaner experiment to run together.

As Tess drafts that control, Ruth asks one plain mathematical question and
exposes a real flaw in its logic. Tess stops, checks the design, and sees that
Ruth is right. She revises the protocol and brings the new version back to Ruth
for another look before the run. This is their first exchange as colleagues and
the beginning of a friendship that does not depend on Evan or Ruth's later
lucid intervals.

The exact mathematical question and design flaw remain late-bound. Ruth's
contribution must be sound but ordinary, drawing on habits and knowledge she
already has rather than privileged access to the projector.

After a long garage session, Ruth goes out to the garden when she loses the
thread. Tess follows because she wants air, not because Evan asks her to bring
Ruth back. Ruth points to a volunteer plant and asks Tess to identify it. Tess
gets it wrong, and Ruth teases her. This becomes their first time together
without the experiment supplying the subject and establishes humor as part of
their friendship before care becomes part of it.

The exact plant, Tess's wrong identification, and Ruth's joke remain
late-bound.

As the audit and Ruth's requested repeat runs lengthen, Tess begins sleeping in
the spare room instead of driving home after every late session. She brings
Mouse and carries the cat's food, litter supplies, and other necessities back
and forth.

Before the invitation, Ruth notices Tess looking at Evan when she thinks he is
occupied with the work. Ruth later catches Evan looking at Tess in the same
way. Ruth keeps the resulting private joy to herself.

Ruth eventually asks why Tess keeps taking everything home and tells her to
leave it. Tess does. She keeps her apartment for a while, but the balance of
her mornings, work, and ordinary life shifts to Ruth's house until it is where
she lives. There is no formal moving day.

This begins as a practical extension of the work and Tess's friendship with
Ruth. Ruth is not arranging the romance, but her invitation isn't exactly sly,
and it isn't exactly not either. Her private pleasure in the mutual attraction
she has noticed gives the practical invitation a second warmth without reducing
Ruth to Tess's way into Evan's house.

Tess and Evan's professional friction supplies the first romantic turn. During
a long argument over one of the controls, Tess realizes that the technical
point no longer explains why she is enjoying the argument so much. She kisses
him before either of them concedes.

The kiss does not resolve the technical question or convert disagreement into
foreplay every time they work. They return to the disputed point later, and
their ability to remain separate while arguing continues to matter. The exact
control, argument, physical setting, and immediate words remain late-bound.

Tess tells Evan about the ferry later. His description of her survival as a
statistical catastrophe with one living remainder deepens an existing
relationship rather than initiating it.

As Tess tells him, her age and the outline of the disaster connect her to the
child survivor Evan remembers from news coverage when he was thirteen. He says
so immediately. Tess knows from that conversation that he remembers the public
story, and she tells him what it omitted and how its miracle framing turned her
family into background.

Evan's immediate disclosure belongs to the trust they have at this point. His
later breach begins only after he suspects Tess's causal rarity may matter and
secretly seeks the detailed ferry, rescue, and medical records. The exact news
account, remembered images, and parts Tess corrects remain late-bound.

Tess then designs a precommitted control that removes the dependency she found,
uses independent inputs, and gives the result a real consequence in the next
run. A new chain of ordinary events satisfies the registered condition before
the cutoff. This is the first external anomaly Tess cannot explain away.

The operation has not noticed Tess or answered a challenge. Her control places
a fresh consequential branch inside the live apparatus loop, and the mindless
operation selects a physically valid history that closes it. The exact hidden
dependency, control inputs, registered condition, consequence, and event chain
remain late-bound.

Nobody tells Tess about Evan's childhood memory of the ferry story or recruits
her because she survived it. Her history plays no role in Lena's request.

The exact coincidence chain that first interests Tess and the flaws she finds
in its record remain open.

The exact hospital, injuries, length of recovery, guardianship transition, and
sequence by which she learns each death remain open.

## What Tess notices during Evan's secret measurement

Once Evan begins treating Tess's choices as a separate signal, his questions
around small decisions become more particular. He waits for her answers and
sometimes records them afterward. Tess notices the added attention, but it has
an ordinary explanation. Evan already logs nearly everything, her judgments
legitimately affect the work, and they are newly intimate. She reads the
questions as documentation mixed with genuine interest.

Tess does not discover the separate signal, the causal-rarity hypothesis, or
Evan's search for detailed ferry, rescue, and medical records. He admits those
things voluntarily after paired loss and echo becomes conclusive.

Her first response is, "What exactly did you use?" Tess asks for scope before
motive. Evan begins with the ferry, rescue, and medical records. He tells her
which records he searched, what facts he took from them, and how he used those
facts to estimate the rarity of her survival. Only then does he name the
ordinary choices he isolated and which garage observations and runs that work
affected. He understands where the deepest violation lies and does not need
Tess to force him past a technical account. The exact documents and details he
names remain late-bound.

Evan's copies of the records, his model and calculations, and the Tess-specific
choice logs remain on a garage workstation and local storage separate from the
apparatus controller. He sends none of it, including any derived Tess-specific
conclusion, to Lena or the participating laboratories. It does not enter the
proof-to-stop protocol. Evan uses it only to guide his private interpretation
and local garage runs before he confesses. The workstation and storage are not
part of the active core he later moves to the remote site.

The first record Evan names is the official rescue timeline. He begins to
recite its search attempts, failed approaches, the temporary path that let
rescuers reach Tess, her extraction, and the attempts to reach the rest of her
family.

"I know the timeline," Tess says. "Tell me what you did with it."

Tess has studied the document herself. She does not need Evan to repeat the
chain by which she lost everyone. She makes him account for his own action.
Evan tells her that he used the sequence to model the rescue bottleneck that
left Tess alive when the others were not. The medical records then let him
estimate how rarely a child with Tess's exposure would survive profound
hypothermia and prolonged resuscitation without a different neurological
outcome.

"Show me," Tess says.

Evan opens the complete local folder on the garage workstation. Tess reviews
his copies of the source records, the model and calculations, the timestamped
choice logs, and the notes linking them to particular garage observations and
runs. She verifies the scope of what he used without moving or copying the
folder out of the garage.

Evan stays beside her. He answers every question directly, does not choose the
order in which she opens the files, and does not touch the keyboard unless she
asks him to open something. His presence makes the confession examinable. It
does not repair what he did. The treatment does not need to name the first file
she opens or narrate the review screen by screen. It only needs to establish
that she sees enough to understand the scope.

The exact agency, document title, event timings, access path, and rescue
mechanics remain late-bound.

The admission gives the earlier questions and notes a second meaning. Tess
cannot know which ordinary moments were also observations. This does not make
Evan's interest or their intimacy false. It damages her trust because he let
genuine attention double as undisclosed measurement. The exact small choices
and the first instance Tess notices remain late-bound.

The independent analogue has ruled Tess out as necessary for the basic effect.
It has not answered whether her choices helped the garage lineage become the
carrier that now continues without either of them. Evan's dated work shows
that he suspected this while Tess could still have withdrawn from live runs.
He tells her only after that withdrawal can no longer undo her possible
contribution.

Tess's organizing response is fear, not fury. She is frightened that she may
have helped the anomaly get out of control. She is upset that Evan withheld a
risk concerning her own actions and denied her the chance to decide whether to
continue. His use of her survival records makes the breach personal and gives
their ordinary exchanges a second meaning, but the emotional center is the
possible harm and the lost opportunity to withdraw. Their love remains real,
and the breach seriously damages trust without destroying it.

## First solo contact

Tess's first solo projector contact receives a full narrative turn in the
eventual synopsis. It occurs after repeated runs have accumulated enough
exposure to draw her into contact, but before she and Evan undergo their later
shared contact.

Chronologically, the contact falls after Tess and Evan have become lovers and
after Tess and Mouse have largely moved into the house. It occurs before the
replica-lab disaster and the institutional acceleration that follows. The
encounter therefore belongs to the established household rather than causing
the romance or the move, and it has room to alter Tess's participation before
the project begins killing people.

The encounter establishes Tess's spatio-acoustic, relation-centered perception
on its own terms. Voices, sound, depth, rotation, and her own position within
the change carry the experience. Evan's visual account can provide context,
but it does not translate the contact for her or define what she perceived.

The solo encounter is with the Turning Knot. Tess experiences its impossible
kata-ward turn through sound, depth, rotation, and her own place within the
relation. She returns certain that the contact is real and that Evan's reports
cannot be dismissed as private afterimages. She has no theory of the Knot and
recovers no message or readable motive from it.

The later shared contact lets Evan recognize visually the same invariant Tess
first experienced spatio-acoustically. It also carries the paired-loss proof.
The solo contact does neither of those jobs. Its exact sensory progression
remains open.

## Terminal contribution

Tess identifies the missing requirement in the final shutdown attempt. Any
ending Evan can fully specify, observe, and control still leaves the operation
with one Evan-shaped path it can turn into another cycle. A real ending
requires a second person whose choice remains outside his control and cannot be
checked before he makes his own.

Tess recognizes the shape of that requirement in an ordinary mathematical
principle Ruth used earlier: unlike terms or genuinely independent alternatives
cannot be collapsed merely because someone wants one answer or because they
point toward the same intended result. Ruth's principle helps Tess articulate
why the two choices must remain separate. It does not tell either of them how
to build the terminal apparatus, and Ruth never knows its eventual use. Exact
wording and early scene placement remain late-bound.

This follows from Tess's relation-first understanding of the collapse. Evan
can work out how the powered-down apparatus will receive both choices, but he
cannot supply Tess's part, reduce it to a predicted input, or learn it in time
to condition his own response. Tess supplies the structural requirement. Evan
supplies the physical implementation. Both understand the cost, and Tess can
still refuse.

This decision fixes their roles without fixing the terminal hardware, controls,
outcome states, or failure cases. Those remain deferred until the final chapter
or two are outlined.
