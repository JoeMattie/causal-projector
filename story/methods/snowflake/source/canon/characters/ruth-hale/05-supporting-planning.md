## Narrative distance and organizing spine

Ruth's treatment alternates between close and external third. During lucid or
more accessible periods, the narration can move close enough to follow her
associations, judgments, humor, irritation, and recognition. During more
impaired periods, it observes her through action, physical attention, habit,
speech, and relation to the immediate environment without inventing an inner
continuity she may not possess.

The external passages remain Ruth-centered. They do not enter Evan's, Tess's,
or another observer's interpretation of her, and they do not make impaired
Ruth scenery in someone else's emotional story. A return to close narration
signals available narrative access, not restored personhood.

The full-story spine is **collaboration versus care**. Ruth wants to remain a
colleague, participant, mother, friend, and decision-maker while increasingly
needing practical care. Care is not itself the enemy, and independence is not
the measure of her worth. The conflict sharpens when Evan lets care become
management and lets the work continue to call itself collaboration after it
has stopped waiting for her.

## Opening structure

The treatment begins chronologically with brief touches on the end of Ruth's
working life and Evan's later move into her house. These passages establish her
professional authority, the practical transfer into shared care, and her role
in deciding how that care begins. They remain an overture rather than a long
decline narrative.

The first fully developed sequence begins in the garage with the project
already underway. Ruth reviews a run containing the cold notch and identifies
it again without access to her prior discoveries. This places the treatment at
the novel's active threshold and demonstrates both her surviving mathematical
attention and her broken recent continuity.

The overture does not make Ruth's earlier clarity her authentic self or turn
the garage opening into the aftermath of a completed person. Exact career,
diagnosis, progression, and circumstances of Evan's move remain open.

## Physical decline and terminal staging

Ruth's physical decline develops gradually through ordinary life. Fatigue
first shortens her time in the garage and garden and leaves her sleeping more
often. Standing, stairs, and transitions between rooms then require more time
and increasing help. These changes coexist with the repeated projector-linked
lucid intervals; cognitive clarity does not reverse her physical course.

Meals become smaller before swallowing trouble is named. Tess begins quietly
adapting food and eating beside Ruth while the act can still remain an ordinary
shared meal. By the period after the granddaughter memory and final requested
exposure, Ruth needs more help moving and tires quickly. As the external study
expands, difficulty eating and swallowing becomes explicit, speech requires
more effort, and sleep occupies more of her day.

Ruth is already in an advanced terminal course when Evan moves the apparatus
to the remote shutdown site. The local catastrophe leaves the house standing
but cuts roads, utilities, communications, and emergency access. Tess's failed
attempt to reach emergency help establishes what the isolation removes. The
blast does not cause Ruth's decline, natural lucidity, or death.

Ruth's natural terminal or paradoxical lucidity arrives inside that existing
course after the house is isolated. It gives her coherent presence without
restoring physical strength. The interval yields again to exhaustion,
difficulty speaking and swallowing, intermittent responsiveness,
unconsciousness, and death with Tess and Mouse beside her.

## End of close access

Close-third access ends when Ruth can no longer sustain attention, before she
becomes unconscious. A physical anchor such as Mouse's weight against her or
Tess's hand in hers carries the treatment into Ruth-centered external
narration.

The external passage remains with Ruth's body, responses, immediate
surroundings, and the people beside her without entering Tess's interior. It
can follow intermittent responsiveness, unconsciousness, breathing, and death
without claiming Ruth's inaccessible experience. The shift does not announce
that Ruth has ceased to be a person; it marks the limit of what the narration
can honestly know.

## Ruth leaves Tess free

Ruth asks nothing further of Tess about Tess's relationship with Evan. She
does not ask Tess to forgive him, remain with him, confront his pattern for
him, or protect him from the consequences of his absence. Her only request is
that Tess make sure he knows how much Ruth loves him if he returns too late.

The perspective Ruth gives Tess is therefore a gift of understanding rather
than an assignment. Tess remains free to decide what Evan's pattern means for
her own life. Ruth can love her son, describe him truthfully, and decline to
make another woman responsible for changing him.

## Ruth's message for Evan

Ruth knows she may die before Evan returns. She tells Tess that, if she does,
Tess must make sure Evan knows how much she loves him. The request allows Ruth
to speak to her son through the person who is actually present without
pretending that his absence does not hurt or that he will necessarily arrive
in time.

Ruth then helps Tess place that absence in the history of the man she knows.
Evan has always turned toward work when loss or grief threatens because work
lets him postpone facing either one. Ruth speaks from a lifetime of knowing
her son, not from a complete recovery of every autobiographical detail or a
finished diagnosis of him. Exact memories and dialogue remain late-bound.

What Ruth tells Tess explains how love and absence can coexist in Evan. It
does not make the absence harmless, excuse the choices that brought him away,
or decide what Tess must do with that understanding.

## Scope of the final natural lucidity

Ruth's final natural lucidity restores the immediate truth. She recognizes
Tess and Mouse, knows Evan is away working, and understands that her body is
failing. Her thought and speech become coherent enough for genuine presence
with Tess, within the physical limits already established by her decline.

The interval does not return Ruth's complete autobiography, resolve the
granddaughter history, explain the projector, or make her the authoritative
version of herself. It is natural terminal or paradoxical lucidity rather than
a projector effect. The narration may move close again because Ruth is
available to it, not because every missing part of her has been restored.

## Contact as relational return

All three aspects combine in Ruth's projector contact. The parts of a relation
become available together, so she no longer has to hold one term in mind while
searching for the next. Ruth recognizes that relief as the familiar absorbed
rhythm of following a proof or teaching a class. At the same time, voices,
faces, objects, and her own place among them become easier to locate.

These are layers of one experience rather than three separate effects. The
simultaneous relation is the contact's distinctive texture; mathematical
absorption is Ruth's human recognition of it; and the newly legible room is
its immediate lived consequence. The experience gives her orientation and
relief, not new facts, recovered cosmology, or a complete return of everything
she once knew.

## Three modes of Tess's care

The treatment uses all three recurring household expressions of Tess's care.
They can enter at different points in Ruth's decline rather than forming one
explanatory montage.

- Tess sets out Ruth's medication and water, then turns to an ordinary nearby
  task instead of visibly supervising her. She respects Ruth's dislike of
  being watched while remaining close enough to keep the moment safe.
- When Ruth stalls between places or tasks, Tess does not quiz her or insist on
  correcting her orientation. She says what she is about to do and gives Ruth
  an easy way to move with her.
- Tess quietly adapts a shared meal to what Ruth can manage and eats beside
  her. As Ruth's physical condition worsens, the meals can also foreshadow and
  then reveal increasing difficulty with eating and swallowing.

Together, the moments show Tess responding to Ruth rather than applying one
fixed technique. Ruth recognizes the same patient intent through privacy,
movement, and company.

## Ruth recognizes Tess's care

During Ruth's less-lucid periods, Tess knows intuitively how to approach her.
She attends to Ruth's posture, attention, tone, and immediate surroundings,
then offers what is needed without requiring Ruth to explain her confusion or
prove that she needs help. This intuition grows from familiarity and close
attention rather than a formal protocol.

Tess is genuinely caring for Ruth in some of these moments. The treatment does
not need to rename every act of care as collaboration in order to preserve
Ruth's dignity. Even when Ruth cannot place the whole situation, she can sense
Tess's intent, accept the help, and appreciate her for it. Tess will not always
read her perfectly, and Ruth can still refuse or become irritated, but care is
part of their friendship rather than the thing that replaces it.

## Ruth invites Tess and Mouse home

Before the invitation, Ruth notices the way Tess looks at Evan when she thinks
he is occupied with the work. Ruth later catches Evan looking at Tess in the
same way. The mutual recognition fills Ruth with a private joy she does not
announce to either of them.

Ruth notices that Tess continues carrying Mouse's food, litter supplies, and
other necessities home after each overnight stay even though she and Mouse are
already spending much of their lives in the house. Ruth understands that Tess
still considers herself a guest and needs permission to stop doing so.

Ruth deliberately gives that permission. Telling Tess to leave the supplies is
an invitation to make the house her home, not merely a comment on wasted
effort. The invitation includes Mouse and grows from Ruth's independent
friendship with Tess. Ruth is not arranging their romance, but the invitation
isn't exactly sly, and it isn't exactly not either. She quietly enjoys making
room for what she has already seen. Exact wording remains late-bound.

## Ruth lets the repetition go

When Evan proceeds without her again, Ruth suspects what has happened but
cannot reconstruct enough of the recent sequence to establish it cleanly. She
does not need a note or a recovered memory of his promise. She knows her son,
and she knows how completely work can occupy him. That older, personal
knowledge is enough for her to recognize the likely shape of the event.

Ruth chooses not to confront him again. She lets this instance go rather than
turning her uncertainty into an investigation or demanding that Evan prove a
negative. The decision belongs to Ruth; it is not evidence that the first
exclusion no longer mattered or permission for Evan to continue. The reader
can see the accumulating pattern while Ruth decides this is not the argument
she wants to spend herself on.

## Evan's sincere exception

Evan understands Ruth's objection to the mapping-reversal run, apologizes
sincerely, and promises to wait for her. The apology repairs the immediate
encounter but does not make him recognize the larger habit. After the
granddaughter memory, he proceeds with the non-cognitive run that draws Tess
into solo contact and tells himself that Ruth's request applied to her
exposures rather than the whole project.

Evan is not lying to Ruth when he apologizes, and the later exclusion is not a
secret decision to disregard her. He treats it as a categorically different
run rather than recognizing the repeated choice. That distinction preserves
his love and his responsibility at the same time.

## Ruth rejoins the run

Ruth takes up whatever meaningful work remains and helps finish the run. Her
contribution does not need to expose a mistake, rescue the result, or prove that
Evan was wrong to think the apparatus could operate without her. Once the run
is safely complete, she tells him that completing her part for her is not the
same as collaborating with her.

Ruth's response is neither an ultimatum nor a withdrawal from the project. She
wants the work to remain shared and names the condition Evan has violated. Her
ability to make a useful contribution and her right to be included are related
but not interchangeable. Exact technical work and dialogue remain late-bound.

## The project stops waiting

After Tess joins the team but before Ruth's first lucid interval, Ruth wakes to
find a mapping-reversal confirmation already under way. Evan began while she
slept because the ring had reached a narrow usable state, and the pre-run check
that belonged to Ruth has already been completed or bypassed.

The recognition is immediate and physical rather than reconstructed from a
notebook or explained to her by Tess. The apparatus is live, the work has
advanced, and Ruth can see that no one expected her participation before the
decision became irreversible. This is the first concrete instance in her
treatment of Evan letting the project call itself collaboration after it has
stopped waiting for her. It does not require Ruth to summarize that pattern in
thematic language.

## Ruth's use of the recovered interval

Ruth completes the minimum safety checks agreed for the final requested
exposure, then ends her participation in the testing. She decides the remaining
clarity belongs to her and spends it away from the apparatus with Tess and
Mouse. The exact ordinary activity remains open, with the garden, kitchen, or
another familiar part of the house available.

The choice is not a rejection of mathematics, collaboration, or Evan. It is a
decision about what the benefit is for. Evan may still be completing the run's
technical closeout, but Ruth does not let the remaining interval become a
longer cognitive battery or another attempt to settle the granddaughter
memory.

The interval fades during ordinary time. It is not staged as a final goodbye,
and nobody yet knows it will be Ruth's last projector-linked lucidity. A
concrete anchor such as Mouse's weight or soil on Ruth's hands can carry the
shift back toward external narration.

## Final requested projector exposure

The team gives Ruth one more reduced exposure under her terms. Autobiographical
agreement with Evan's records is removed from the target and from the

The run produces another genuine but temporary lucid interval. It does not

interval is Ruth's last projector-linked lucidity and remains distinct from
the natural terminal or paradoxical lucidity before her death.

Because the run no longer exists to locate Evan's concordance window, Ruth's

## Response to the concordance window

Ruth still wants another lucid interval. The earlier benefits were real to her,
and the incompatible granddaughter memory does not make her reject the clarity
she experienced. She does reject Evan's proposal to define a successful result
as autobiographical agreement with his records.

Ruth distinguishes consent to another reduced exposure from consent to a
protocol that selects against her contradictory memory. She will agree only if
the run does not treat conformity to Evan's history as evidence that she is
restored or herself. Her position does not prove the memory true, settle the
science, or require Lena to consider another exposure safe. Exact wording and
the practical conditions Ruth asks for remain late-bound.

## Continuity transitions

The treatment crosses Ruth's memory gaps through concrete anchors. A mark on a
graph, Mouse's weight, soil on Ruth's hands, a medication cup, Evan's voice, or
another immediate physical fact can persist while narration shifts between
close and external third. The reader can follow that fact even when Ruth cannot
recover the elapsed sequence around it.

Close narration returns when Ruth finds a usable relation again. The anchor
does not restore the missing interval or certify which state is more authentic.
Anchors vary with the scene rather than becoming one symbolic token or a fixed
transition formula. Ruth may also use notes and routines when that is what she
would ordinarily do, but every lapse does not become an investigation.
