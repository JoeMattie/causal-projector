# Mouse: full character synopsis

Status: provisional
Snowflake step: 05

This treatment follows the whole story through Mouse's changing places,
familiar people, routines, and immediate senses. It is not accepted wording.

## Synopsis

Mouse is born in the loft of a cabin in Fairbanks, to a mother she does not
remember and a cabin owner who never knows she exists. Her first world is
rough boards, dust, warm fur, and footsteps moving below her. It does not last.

By the time she is seven, Mouse has bopped from one home to the next and spent
a prolonged stretch living in the woods around Ester. Frostbite has taken the
tips of both ears. She arrives at the shelter tiny beneath a long
black-and-brown coat, interested in every person and animal who passes her,
and unafraid of any of them.

Tess adopts her. The shortened ears look vaguely like a mouse's, so Mouse gets
her name. When Tess moves to the Pacific Northwest, Mouse goes with her.
Interior Alaska gives way to rain, wet plants, unfamiliar animals, and the
salt smell that follows Tess home from the coast.

For the next ten years, Tess is the fixed part of a life that still contains a
lot of movement. Mouse follows her anywhere she is allowed to go, usually
curled inside a little bag or perched on her shoulder. In a new room, she
watches from the arm of a chair until the room becomes ordinary. In a place
without a chair, she finds a corner with a view. She is interested in everyone
without needing to greet them, and affectionate without making a performance
of it.

Evan's garage begins as one more place Tess carries her. It has cold metal,
warm electronics, fans, clicks, unfamiliar animals passing through the yard,
and several humans who spend a great deal of time staring at things that do
not move. Mouse takes the corner of the workbench.

Ruth is the first person there whom Mouse chooses for herself. Ruth sits for
long stretches and has a lap with room in it. Some days she greets Mouse at
once. On others, she studies the little cat as if they have not met. Mouse
climbs onto her either way. When she wants Ruth's attention, she looks up and
meows once. Ruth usually answers with a word or a hand on her back.

During the first run that leaves Ruth alert and speaking easily, Mouse hears
the change before the people stop talking long enough to name it. Ruth's voice
becomes steadier. Her hand moves along Mouse's back without stopping halfway.
The humans gather around her, excited and careful at once. Later the change
fades, and Ruth sleeps with Mouse against her. Each time Ruth returns to this
steadier state, Mouse accepts her. Each time it fades, Mouse accepts her again.

Tess begins staying at the house. The bag is unpacked more often than it is
carried, and the garage, garden, kitchen, and Ruth's chair become Mouse's
regular circuit. Tess and Evan begin sleeping in the same room. Evan becomes
another familiar voice, another hand that knows where she likes to be
scratched, and another person worth watching from the workbench while he
forgets the rest of the room.

The apparatus makes the humans behave strangely. Sometimes one of them goes
still while awake. Sometimes Evan returns from a run moving with unusual
certainty, then cannot explain where he has been. Tess and Evan eventually
enter one of these intervals together and come back responding to the same
absent thing in different ways. Mouse stays near enough to watch until the
strange motions become another part of the room.

The work spreads beyond the garage. New voices come through speakers, people
arrive and stop arriving, and the regular hours of the house disappear.
Mouse's world stays with Tess, Ruth, Evan, the rooms they share, and the
changing ways they move through them.

Ruth grows weaker outside the clear intervals. Her lap becomes harder to
reach. She spends more time asleep and less time touching Mouse once she has
settled. Mouse adjusts by curling against her side or fitting into the space
between her arm and the chair.

After Tess and Evan return from another strange interval, their voices change
with each other. Tess stays close to Ruth. Evan takes the cold apparatus, its
ring, and most of its familiar sounds away from the garage. The house becomes
quieter for a little while.

Then light fills the windows. The floor moves hard enough to throw loose
objects across the room. A blow passes through the house, and the familiar
world outside becomes noise, smoke, broken glass, and silence where distant
human sounds used to be. The garage is gone. Mouse survives without injury.

Ruth's voice becomes clear once more after the house is cut off from everyone
else. Tess stays beside her. Mouse settles close enough to feel Ruth breathe
until the breaths grow shallow and stop. She looks up and meows once. Ruth
does not answer.

Evan returns carrying the smells of the road, cold metal, and dust. He finds
Tess, Ruth, and Mouse together, and stays with them. For a while, Mouse's world
contracts to the two remaining people sorting the objects that still smell
like the one who is gone.

When Tess leaves the ruined neighborhood, Mouse goes with her. She rides in
the same bag and on the same shoulder she has trusted since Alaska. Evan comes
too. Roads close, engines stop, and human voices vanish from devices that once
made them at all hours. One night, the people within hearing speak the same
sounds at the same instant. Later, white flashes cross the sky one after
another. Tess holds Mouse closer each time the ground or the people around
them do something they have never done before.

They reach Lena at a remote observatory. Mouse knows her scent and voice from
the garage. No matter who speaks to her, Lena answers in the same cadence and
repeats the same small set of movements. Evan sits beside her instead of
returning to the instruments. Mouse watches from Tess's bag.

The ground begins to move in long waves. Low sounds travel through the
building from every direction. After that, the rooms themselves stop agreeing
about how they join. A doorway can still be crossed. A floor can still be
followed one paw at a time. But the place reached does not always belong where
the place left behind says it should.

Mouse follows the surfaces under her, Tess's voice, Evan's movement, and the
familiar smells that remain. She crosses a region neither human can place in
the same way and settles. Evan can see where she is. Tess can hear her claws
against the surface and locate her through the sound. Both respond to the same
small animal in the same moment.

The distant place where Evan left the apparatus has become part of the broken
space around the observatory. The old sounds return: fans, relay clicks, metal
shifting, and the movements Evan makes when he is working. Tess and Evan speak
to each other carefully. Mouse hears their familiar voices without knowing
what they are deciding. The broken space separates them until neither can
check what the other does next.

The apparatus changes. Evan and Tess speak their final words. Mouse hears the
cadence, watches both bodies turn, and feels something arrive through a place
that no longer has one direction. The light reaches everything at once.

Mouse watches.
