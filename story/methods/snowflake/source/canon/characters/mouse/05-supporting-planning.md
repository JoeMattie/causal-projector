# Mouse: supporting planning

Status: accepted
Snowflake step: 05

## Narrative distance

- Mouse's treatment uses close external third in the present tense.
- The opening may state objective facts such as Fairbanks, Ester, the shelter,
  and Tess's move for the reader. After that frame, attention stays with
  places, bodies, voices, scents, surfaces, routines, and immediate sensation.
- The treatment may name people and events for clarity without giving Mouse
  human categories, causal theories, or retrospective wisdom.
- Mouse does not need a human false belief, epiphany, or moral explanation.
  Her continuity comes from the people and places she recognizes.

## Accepted early life

- Mouse is born in the loft of a cabin in Fairbanks. Her mother is gone before
  Mouse retains a lasting memory of her, and the cabin's owner never knows
  Mouse exists.
- During her first seven years, Mouse bops from one temporary home to another
  and spends a prolonged stretch in the woods around Ester.
- Frostbite destroys the tips of her ears before she reaches the shelter.
- Tess adopts Mouse from the shelter when Mouse is seven. The shortened ears
  give Tess the basis for her name.
- Tess is about twenty-eight. She moves from Alaska to the Pacific Northwest
  with Mouse, fixing the move in her late twenties rather than leaving its
  timing attached only to the earlier college beat.
- Mouse is seventeen in the main story, so she and Tess have lived together
  for about ten years.
- The exact temporary homes, outdoor sequence, rescue, shelter, adoption scene,
  and interval between adoption and the move remain open.

## Organizing spine

Mouse begins as an animal who belongs to places only briefly and is not even
known to the human who owns the cabin where she is born. Tess becomes the
first lasting constant in her life. Mouse then accumulates an ordinary set of
shared places and people around Tess, Evan, Ruth, the garage, and the house.
As the story destroys those places and finally destroys stable location
itself, Mouse continues finding her people through the local cues available to
her.

This supports her last-reference function without turning the early hardship
into destiny. Mouse can cross the final damaged region because a locally
traversable surface remains under each step and because following Tess through
unfamiliar places is already her ordinary life. She has no special access to
the geometry.

## Selected story beats

1. Birth in the Fairbanks cabin loft, unstable early homes, the Ester woods,
   shelter, adoption at seven, and the move with Tess.
2. Ten years of ordinary travel with Tess in the little bag or on her shoulder.
3. Arrival at Evan's garage, late sessions becoming nights in the spare room,
   Ruth telling Tess to leave Mouse's supplies, and Mouse choosing Ruth as her
   preferred resting companion.
4. Ruth's alternating clarity and decline, experienced through voice, touch,
   sleep, and the familiar single meow.
5. Tess moving into the house, Evan becoming one of Mouse's people, and Mouse
   watching contact without understanding it.
6. Evan kissing Ruth on the top of the head, petting Mouse, saying, "Love you,
   I'll be back," and removing the active core, followed by the local
   catastrophe, Ruth's final natural lucidity and death, and Mouse's unexplained
   survival.
7. Travel with Tess and Evan through mass speech, the orbital catastrophe,
   communications loss, and the journey to Lena.
8. Lena's fixed-successor collapse experienced as repeated cadence and action,
   followed by the geologic catastrophe.
9. Loss of stable geometry, Mouse's crossing and settlement as the last shared
   reference, the humans' final independent choices, and the incoming Big Bang
   from literal animal viewpoint.

## Information boundaries

- Mouse cannot know what the apparatus does, why laboratories exist, what the
  casualties mean, why Tess and Evan argue, what Lena refuses, or what the
  final choice costs.
- She can notice that a familiar person stops coming, voices change, routines
  break, people become still or move oddly, machinery leaves and returns, and
  human attention converges on an event.
- Her calm attention follows her established temperament. It never confirms
  supernatural perception, protection, causal rarity, or selection by the
  operation.
- Her early survival in Alaska and survival of the garage catastrophe remain
  facts of her life, not evidence that she is invulnerable or cosmically
  favored.
- The later clinical observer and child-side reconstruction fall outside
  Mouse's experience and do not belong in her treatment.

## Provisional draft choices

- The draft pays off Mouse's ordinary single meow by having her use it after
  Ruth stops breathing and receive no answer. This is provisional synopsis
  wording, not yet an accepted scene action.
- The draft ends with Mouse watching the incoming event after Evan and Tess
  turn toward it. Exact final words, bodily placement, light, sound, pressure,
  heat, and the destruction of the parent history remain late-bound.
- The exact surface Mouse crosses and the point where she settles remain
  late-bound. The synopsis uses only the locked result that Evan can identify
  her visually and Tess can locate her through sound and relation.

## Terminal boundaries

- Mouse supplies the last shared external fact before Evan and Tess choose.
- She does not reveal, influence, coordinate, trigger, permit, or measure
  either choice.
- The final portion of the genesis-operation chapter uses her literal animal
  viewpoint. It cannot turn into simplified human narration.
- She hears the humans' final words without understanding them and registers
  their attention and bodily response to what arrives.
- Mouse does not understand genesis and does not cross into the child universe
  as an intact animal.
