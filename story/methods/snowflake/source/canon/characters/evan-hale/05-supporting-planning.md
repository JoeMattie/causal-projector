# Evan Hale: Step 5 supporting planning

Status: accepted
Snowflake step: 05

This file preserves locked rationale, information boundaries, change maps,
late-bound notes, and the completed decision record behind the synopsis. It
does not replace the deliverable. The synopsis remains provisional.

## Expanded planning

Locked narrative distance:

- Evan's treatment uses evolving close third in the present tense.
- The narration stays within what Evan knows and believes at each point in the
  story. His engineering habits and self-justifications shape what it treats
  as relevant.
- Irony can emerge from what Evan notices, excuses, or leaves out. The
  narration cannot use his later epiphany to explain his earlier choices for
  him.
- The treatment is neither a first-person confession nor a retrospective
  account from the terminal Evan.

Locked early awareness:

- Before the incompatible granddaughter memory, Evan notices each time he
  proceeds without Ruth, but he does not recognize the incidents as one
  accumulating pattern of abandonment.
- He treats each choice as temporary and technically justified. A clean run
  now seems more useful to Ruth than waiting for her, especially when the
  result might let him return with something worth sharing or something that
  could help her.
- The narration can make the repetition and growing distance visible while
  Evan still experiences each decision as a separate reasonable exception.
- This preserves his responsibility without making him cold enough to accept
  Ruth's neglect as an explicit cost.

Locked organizing spine:

- Ruth remains the emotional spine of Evan's treatment through the entire
  story. Her death does not end her structural place in his account.
- Before Ruth dies, the treatment measures Evan's work against the living
  person beside him. Afterward, her absence measures whether his refusal,
  search for Lena, and final trust in Tess amount to a real change.
- Tess does not replace Ruth. She is the independent person with whom Evan has
  one final chance not to repeat his habit of deciding what love requires on
  someone else's behalf.
- The projector operation supplies the pressure and causal escalation, but it
  does not displace Ruth as the treatment's organizing relationship.

Locked central hinge:

- Ruth's incompatible granddaughter memory and Evan's concordance-window
  response form the central hinge of his treatment.
- The memory alone is not the turn. The turn is Evan answering a lucid,
  coherent Ruth who disagrees with his history by defining a correct result as
  the version of Ruth whose autobiography agrees with him.
- In Evan's immediate account, concordance is a necessary control that might
  preserve the benefit while removing an apparent overshoot. The treatment
  can make the moral change legible without giving him that language yet.
- This is where his pattern shifts from letting work displace Ruth to using
  the work to decide which Ruth counts.

Locked first clarity reward:

- Evan's treatment keeps one compact technical beat for the first externally
  validated result of his clarity.
- Near zero speed, the motor controller has two valid electrical-angle
  possibilities and insufficient back electromotive force to distinguish
  them. Evan realizes the estimator must preserve both until the signal
  returns rather than force one.
- The fix holds up after the clarity fades. This gives Evan a sound reason to
  trust the state and claim the insight as his own.
- The parallel between preserving alternatives in the motor controller and
  destroying them through the projector remains visible to the reader but
  unstated by Evan.
- Employer details, implementation detail beyond the two hypotheses, and the
  surrounding work scene remain outside the treatment unless later needed.

Locked pre-hinge balance:

- The treatment uses three technical strokes before the central hinge.
  Evan reinstalls the defective ring and reproduces the cold notch, his
  motor-control fix survives outside the clarity state, and fresh randomness
  favors a physical consequence only when it controls a live action.
- The live-consequence result gives Evan a reason to call Lena. The treatment
  compresses the mapping reversals, blinded operator-effect work, and later
  rare-target validation rather than reproducing the full control ladder.
- The saved space belongs to Tess's arrival as an independent auditor and to
  Ruth's requested lucid intervals. Evan must experience the team as having
  good reason to continue before the granddaughter contradiction.
- This balance preserves Evan's technical attention without allowing
  laboratory procedure to displace the relationship the treatment is about.

Locked post-hinge balance:

- The treatment uses four post-hinge turns.
- After Lena refuses, Evan sponsors the proof-to-stop test through consortium
  approval. Participating sites run it under local authority, establishing that
  the distributed system can continue without him or the garage apparatus.
- Paired loss and echo reveals erased histories. Evan admits using Tess's
  choices and records, then worsens the admission by calling their meeting
  inevitable. Her trust breaks.
- Evan leaves Tess with Ruth while he pursues another stop. He returns after
  Ruth's death and the local catastrophe, sees Mouse unhurt, and does not turn
  any part of the loss into another investigation. He stays.
- The journey to Lena and the terminal choice test whether that refusal is a
  durable change. Evan seeks Lena as a person, honors her last refusal, and
  finally gives Tess a choice he cannot inspect or replace. Mouse remains
  present as the last shared external reference.
- Technical carrier detail and cosmic escalation remain legible through these
  decisions without taking over the treatment.

Locked return epiphany:

- When Evan returns after Ruth's death, he briefly recognizes the same motion
  in the miscarriage planter, the collapse of his marriage, and the time he
  has lost with Ruth. Pain arrives, and he turns away from the person beside
  him to work on it.
- He does not deliver a full thematic explanation or arrive at a finished
  theory of himself. He recognizes what beginning another investigation would
  mean.
- His refusal makes the recognition consequential. He does not inspect the
  crater, salvage the apparatus, explain Mouse's survival, or ask Tess to help
  him reconstruct the chain. He stays.
- This recognition begins a real change but does not prove it complete. The
  journey to Lena and his inability to inspect Tess's final choice still have
  to test it.
- The recognition does not redeem Ruth's lost time, his marriage, the
  miscarriage, or any erased history.

Locked confession understanding:

- Evan knows that using Tess's choices and survival records without her
  informed consent was a personal violation rather than only a flaw in the
  experiment.
- He confesses because continued concealment would repeat that violation.
- He still believes that complete disclosure and a correct causal explanation
  can restore honest footing between them. He mistakes transparency after the
  fact for a partial restoration of the agency he already took.
- When disclosure does not repair Tess's trust, he reaches for inevitability.
  He treats their meeting and love as an explanation that might make the harm
  intelligible, and instead turns her losses into reasons the violation had to
  happen.
- His honesty matters, but it neither repairs the breach nor places him ahead
  of the recognition he reaches after Ruth's death.

Locked post-incident understanding:

- Evan accepts both casualties as real. He does not minimize the death or
  dismiss the survivor's selective autobiographical loss.
- He classifies them differently. The death is an engineering and containment
  failure. The memory loss is specific enough to trouble him, but the physical
  trauma leaves it medically confounded.
- His compliance with the evidence hold is sincere. He powers down the garage
  because continuing under the failed safeguards would be indefensible, even
  though nobody can force him to stop.
- Once the investigation permits a bounded restart, Evan believes continued
  measurement under stronger controls is the responsible way to find the
  failure and prevent another death.
- This is another form of his central mistake. Faced with grief and
  uncertainty, he converts them into a better procedure. The procedure is
  reasonable enough that he does not yet have to see the pattern in himself.
- He does not privately know that the survivor's loss is cosmological. The
  later paired loss and echo remains the point at which medical ambiguity
  fails and erasure becomes the best explanation.

Locked selected-site role:

- Evan joins the selected-site restart as a technical adviser. He has full
  access to shared measurements, controller logs, and containment records.
- He helps compare runs, interpret anomalies, and design safeguards. He does
  not chair the consortium, operate a participating site's apparatus, or hold
  unilateral authority to approve a consequential run.
- Local teams retain physical control of their systems. Consequential tests
  require the consortium's shared approval under the containment plan.
- When Lena refuses the proof-to-stop test, Evan formulates and sponsors the
  proposal through that approval process. Other researchers and institutions
  authorize and execute it. He does not hack a replica, bypass a hard trip, or
  restart the garage apparatus.
- Lena can refuse her participation and endorsement, but she does not hold a
  unilateral veto over the other sites. Evan's decision to proceed remains a
  breach of their collaboration even though it follows formal procedure.
- The institutional route is part of the trap. The proposal, review,
  approvals, site actions, measurements, and containment responses can become
  components of the live carrier they are trying to test.
- Exact committee structure, vote, participating site, and experimental
  implementation remain late-bound.

Locked proof-to-stop disclosure:

- Evan's proposal states the carrier hypothesis and the recursive danger
  plainly. The test result will determine containment or decommissioning
  actions, and that live response may strengthen or complete the suspected
  carrier.
- The proposal also records Lena's refusal and her reason for it. Evan does not
  recast her objection as a generic request for more caution.
- Consortium reviewers, participating site leads, and the operators who will
  execute the test receive the same material before approval. Uncertainty is
  marked as uncertainty rather than softened or concealed.
- They approve knowingly. Suspicion alone will not sustain permanent shutdown,
  and separate sites making uncoordinated choices may create a worse risk than
  one bounded test under the common containment plan.
- Their consent is ethically real. It does not protect the decision from
  becoming a live input to the operation. The informed review, approval, site
  actions, and response are the very structure the test is meant to examine.
- Evan treats full disclosure and collective approval as evidence that the
  test is legitimate. This preserves a distinction from his secret use of
  Tess's choices and records, even though both decisions express his faith that
  the right procedure can justify another consequential intervention.
- Exact briefing language, review sequence, vote, and operator discussion
  remain late-bound.

Locked proof-to-stop response:

- When the test succeeds, Evan understands the result correctly. The informed
  approval process and the participating sites' response were already parts of
  the carrier, and the test strengthened the system it proved.
- His first reaction is grim vindication rather than triumph or immediate
  remorse. Lena was right about the recursive danger. Evan was right that the
  institutions would not sustain permanent shutdown without proof.
- He still believes seeking the proof was necessary. In his account, the test
  was dangerous, but ignorance and fragmented site decisions would have been
  worse.
- He moves directly into coordinated shutdown work. He wants to map the live
  links, prevent further consequential feedback, and give each site a
  decommissioning path that does not create another response cycle.
- He does not yet regret sponsoring the test. Its actionable result lets him
  treat the damage as the cost of learning enough to act, which is another
  version of converting helplessness into work.
- This is not his epiphany. The later erasure proof changes what he thinks the
  operation costs, and Ruth's death finally changes what he does when another
  investigation presents itself.
- Exact reporting sequence, shutdown plan, and first post-result action remain
  late-bound.

Locked understanding of Lena's rupture:

- Evan expects the collaboration to survive the test. He disclosed Lena's
  objection accurately, the result proved her concern correct, and he now sees
  coordinated containment as a duty they share.
- He expects anger, damaged trust, and a hard argument. He does not expect Lena
  to end their active work together.
- When she withdraws from every live response, Evan recognizes that she has
  drawn a moral boundary. He does not claim that she is confused or afraid.
- He nevertheless experiences the departure as leaving when the work matters
  most. In his account, the dangerous test has created an obligation to help
  contain what it proved.
- He does not attack Lena publicly or misstate her refusal. His mistake is
  expecting accurate acknowledgment and a shared problem to preserve a
  relationship after he acted across its boundary.
- He cannot yet understand her withdrawal as a complete, non-instrumental
  refusal. His later change must alter that reading before he can honor her
  final refusal at the observatory.
- Exact exchange, timing, and practical separation remain late-bound.

Locked later understanding of Lena's refusal:

- Paired loss and echo proves that Lena's warning reached beyond ordinary
  experimental risk, but Evan still responds by pursuing the distributed stop
  chain. The new knowledge does not yet let him understand her refusal from
  inside.
- He understands only after Ruth's death, when he returns and refuses to
  inspect the crater, recover the ring, or begin another investigation.
- Making his own first non-instrumental refusal changes his reading of Lena's
  departure. She did not abandon necessary work. She refused a definition of
  duty in which every attempt to stop had to become another live input.
- This recognition belongs to the same moral change as staying with Tess and
  Ruth's absence. It is not a separate explanatory epiphany, and it does not
  repair the collaboration.
- At the observatory, Evan proves that the understanding lasts. When Lena
  refuses one final test while she can still make a distinct choice, he honors
  the refusal and stays with her.
- Exact recollection, internal wording, and any acknowledgment to Lena remain
  late-bound.

Locked final unknowability:

- Ruth's interior experience remains the organizing limit of Evan's
  understanding through the end of his treatment.
- He never learns what the lucid intervals felt like to her, whether she
  experienced them as rescue, or which autobiography she understood as her
  own.
- The surviving records can establish disagreement but cannot authenticate a
  correct Ruth. Entity contact, the later erasure proof, and the terminal
  cosmology do not supply a posthumous answer.
- Evan can understand that his concordance window forced agreement with his
  history. He cannot work backward from that recognition and recover Ruth's
  unmeasured experience.
- His change is that he stops trying to define which Ruth counted. He carries
  the uncertainty without converting it into another test, explanation, or
  claim on her behalf.
- Tess's unobserved final choice and the operation's terminal status remain
  real uncertainties, but they do not replace Ruth as the emotional limit of
  his account.
- The treatment cannot give Ruth a final message, retrospective approval, or
  cosmological authority to resolve Evan's guilt.

Locked change map:

- **Care:** Evan begins by treating useful intervention as presence. He later
  uses concordance to decide which Ruth counts. After her death, care becomes
  staying without extracting another result.
- **Proof:** He first treats proof as the condition for responsible action.
  Proof-to-stop shows that obtaining an answer can strengthen the thing being
  measured. He still chooses more work until his first refusal.
- **Consent:** He mistakes Ruth's requested lucidity for permission to define
  success around his history and uses Tess without informed consent. Full
  consortium disclosure shows that valid consent matters but cannot make a
  recursive causal loop safe. At the end, he preserves Tess's independent
  choice without inspecting it.
- **Stopping:** He first understands stopping as a procedure that must produce
  evidence. He misreads Lena's refusal as withdrawal, then understands it only
  after making his own refusal without demanding a result.

Already locked:

- Evan begins loving, competent, sad, and convinced that useful work is how he
  remains with people.
- He mistakes reduced ambiguity for truth and treats each useful result as a
  reason for another intervention.
- His story turns when the shared garage project advances without Ruth, when
  Ruth's incompatible memory becomes a calibration problem, and when he uses
  Lena's and Tess's resistance as inputs to continued work.
- His first sincere refusal comes too late to stop the independent operation.
  He stays after Ruth's death rather than investigate the catastrophe.
- The journey to Lena tests whether he can act usefully without converting a
  person into a project.
- At the terminal boundary, he gives Tess the full truth and accepts that he
  cannot inspect, model, coerce, or replace her decision.
- Mouse belongs in his domestic and terminal story without becoming evidence
  he can interpret.
