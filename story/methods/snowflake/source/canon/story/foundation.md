# Story foundation

Status: accepted

## Working title

Open. `Causal Flux`, `The Causal Projector`, and other earlier labels remain
working names only.

## Intended form and tone

- Hard science fiction escalating into ontological horror and cosmic tragedy.
- Conceptually dense, clear, dry, intimate, and technically literate.
- Similar ambitions to qntm-style fiction, but do not imitate any living
  author's prose.
- The science must generate character choices and consequences rather than
  becoming a sequence of impressive laboratory reports.

## Core tragic engine

Evan does not begin by trying to cure Ruth. He is already living with her and
caring for her. The garage project gives them something to work on and talk
about while she begins to disappear. He never seriously expects it to restore
her mind.

The tragedy begins when the project produces real evidence that it might.

Evan's fatal strategy is to turn helplessness, grief, and love into work. He
believes that labor is how he proves care. The machine rewards exactly this
pathology by making his labor effective, euphoric, and increasingly
inevitable.

The novel's private thematic sentence is:

> Evan cannot accept helpless presence as a form of love.

## Opening and timeline

- Begin with Evan and Ruth already working in the garage, near the point when
  the experiment becomes strange.
- Do not open with his move into the house, the end of his marriage, or a long
  apparatus origin story.
- Reveal backstory through present objects, habits, flashbacks, and memory.
- The opening project is an ordinary engineering attempt to create a
  persistent flux source for portable or open low-field MRI without liquid
  helium.
- Evan has built an air liquefier using a cryocooler salvaged from an old
  cellular rack, a detail drawn from Joe's real experience.

The garage initially represents companionship. Later Evan abandons Ruth
emotionally while remaining in the same room with the machine. The kitchen
remains useful for medication, meals, caregiving friction, practical
independence, and the parts of life that do not fit the project.
