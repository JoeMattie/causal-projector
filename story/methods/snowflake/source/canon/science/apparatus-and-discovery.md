# Apparatus and discovery

Status: accepted

## The apparatus

Real-science ancestry:

- melt-textured YBCO high-temperature superconductor;
- an LN2 bath or stage that holds the YBCO below its superconducting
  transition, without a liquid-helium magnet system;
- type-II vortices and flux pinning;
- a thermally actuated traveling-wave flux pump;
- a Prussian-blue magnetic material whose permeability changes with
  temperature;
- a moving heat pulse producing a moving magnetic-reluctance / field profile;
- repeated vortex injection and pinning;
- an asymmetric coherent-plus-lossy YBCO structure;
- rotating or traveling RF magnetic drive;
- ordinary energy and entropy costs.

Fictional leap:

- a material defect and control-system phase edge cause the ordinary
  electromagnetic cycle nearly to close while leaving a small geometric
  mismatch in the deeper causal code;
- repeated cycles act like a non-Hermitian projector or power iteration over
  complete histories;
- the live feedback path selects which future components survive.

Opening diagnostic sequence:

- Evan and Ruth are trying to pump enough trapped field into a superconducting
  ring to justify continued work toward a portable imaging device.
- The old defective ring falls just short of that target.
- Glass-bodied silicon PN junction diodes at selected positions around the
  ring monitor the traveling thermal pulse through the Prussian-blue compound.
  They are biased at constant current, and their forward voltage supplies the
  temperature proxy. Exact part number, probe count, and attachment geometry
  remain late-bound.
- As the traveling heat pulse reaches one fixed sector of the defective ring,
  its diode reports a repeatable cold notch below the cold-stage temperature
  while neighboring probes warm normally. Evan assumes an unresolved
  instrumentation fault and replaces the suspected probe and the measurement
  chain through the data logger. He has not yet replaced the computer.
- The expensive custom ring he ordered months earlier finally arrives, meets
  the trapped-field target, and makes the temperature anomaly disappear.
- Late that night, Evan puts the defective ring back into the apparatus. The
  same anomaly returns.
- Evan then advances the pulse timing in an attempt to move the notch toward
  another sensor. During the resulting clarity state, a better phase sequence
  for the ordinary flux-pump cycle becomes obvious to him. It increases the
  trapped field enough to keep the defective ring technically useful.
- The timing change moves the notch between the installed sensors, making the
  original diagnostic appear to disappear. Evan initially treats this as
  successful isolation rather than recognizing that he has moved the
  measurement window.
- The same clarity interval supplies the zero-speed motor-estimator insight
  from his day job. Both the ordinary flux-pump improvement and the motor fix
  survive the altered state, giving him two valid technical reasons to trust
  it before he recognizes that the garage run caused it.
- Related altered states recur during later timing work. Their repetition,
  rather than the first experience alone, forces Evan to acknowledge that the
  project is doing something to him.
- The exact depth and duration of the notch remain open. It must defeat known
  cryogenic sensor and material effects without establishing a literal
  violation of thermodynamics. Phase collapse remains a fallback if the
  temperature anomaly fails that standard, but it is not part of the current
  opening.

Podkletnov:

- The novel should explicitly mention the disputed Podkletnov rotating-YBCO
  experiments at some point.
- The garage component is a defective YBCO ring. The composite-disk
  construction belongs to the Podkletnov ancestry; current apparatus language
  must not silently alternate between a disk and the ring.
- They are historical inspiration or a false interpretation, not accepted
  evidence of gravitational shielding.

## Discovery and validation

Strong devices worth preserving from the old outline and draft:

- the well-made replacement YBCO ring meets the engineering target and removes
  the temperature anomaly, while reinstalling the defective ring reproduces
  the anomaly;
- statistically independent sensors lose effective rank and share one
  residual mode;
- the control-system timing fault walks through RF, thermal, and
  vortex-relaxation phase;
- fresh randomness remains fair when logged, ignored, or replayed, but becomes
  biased when it controls a live consequential branch;
- reversing the bit-to-action mapping reverses the raw bit bias while
  preserving the favored physical action;
- a precommitted improbable but physically valid target occurs;
- an impossible target fails through adjacent legal nuisances, heat, and
  saturation rather than law violation;
- blinded independent replication separates the effect from Evan's
  expectation.

These experimental devices are salvageable, but the old chapter sequence is
not binding.

Paragraph 2's first deliberate outcome test is locked at the level needed for
the Step 4 synopsis:

- A fresh physical random bit chooses between two safe, prepared actions in the
  live apparatus. Exact hardware, action pair, run count, and statistical
  threshold remain late-bound.
- Fresh bits remain fair when they are merely recorded or ignored. Replaying
  an earlier bitstream through the same command path produces no effect. Bias
  appears only when a fresh bit controls a real physical consequence in the
  current run.
- Swapping the bit-to-action mapping swaps the raw bit bias while preserving
  the favored physical action.
- The resulting sequence is improbable but physically valid. It establishes
  selection by consequence rather than an intrinsically lucky bit value or a
  faulty random source.
- The later precommitted rare target remains a separate escalation.
