# Esther "Tess" Kertesz

## One-sentence description

Esther "Tess" Kertesz is a catastrophe modeler and the neurologically intact sole survivor of a childhood Alaska ferry disaster, whose near-unique causal history makes her almost impossible for Evan's machine to rewrite and leaves her as the only person capable of loving him without becoming part of him.

## Name

- Full name: Esther Kertesz
- Everyday name: Tess
- People routinely hear or spell `Kertesz` as `Curtis`; she corrects them automatically.
- The surname is Hungarian-American. The unaccented spelling `Kertesz` is canon.

## Age

- 38 during the main story.
- Evan is 43.
- She was eight during the ferry disaster, about thirty years before the novel.
- Evan was thirteen and remembers the simplified news story without initially connecting the child survivor to adult Tess.

## Motivation

To live as a person rather than a miracle and to make something useful of survival without pretending it occurred for a reason.

## Immediate goal

At Lena's request, determine whether the impossible coincidence chains accumulating around Evan are genuine compound anomalies or retrospective pattern-making by a technically gifted crank.

## Active false belief

Trusting another person when she cannot independently verify their choice is negligence disguised as intimacy.

## Deeper philosophy

Assigning meaning to survival is retroactive fraud that turns the dead into necessary scenery in someone else's satisfying story.

## Primary conflict

The projector can smoothly replace ordinary people with nearby compliant variants. Tess has almost no nearby compliant versions. It must preserve her substantially intact or erase her. She therefore remains an independent moral and causal vector while falling in love with a man whose machine is built to eliminate independence.

## Work

Tess works at the fictional **North Pacific Resilience Institute**, an independent nonprofit that builds open catastrophe models for governments, tribal nations, ports, ferry systems, utilities, and humanitarian organizations.

Her specialty is **compound and cascading risk**: events in which individually ordinary failures become catastrophic through weather, infrastructure, human decisions, institutional response, and incomplete data.

Lena recruits her because this expertise is exactly what is needed to distinguish a long selected chain from cherry-picked coincidence.

## Ferry disaster

At eight, Tess is traveling with her immediate family aboard a fictional Alaska Marine Highway-style ferry on an exposed Gulf of Alaska route.

A catastrophic sequence involving severe weather, progressive flooding or structural failure, a partial roll or grounding, an inaccessible maintenance void, an air pocket, profound hypothermia, a temporary rescue path, and prolonged resuscitation leaves Tess the sole neurologically intact survivor.

Her entire immediate family dies.

The exact vessel, route, failure sequence, and rescue mechanics remain open for later technical development. It should be plausible enough that no single miracle carries the entire survival. Her rarity comes from the compound bottleneck and the survival of this substantially continuous adult identity.

## Degree of causal rarity

The disaster alone is not the whole bottleneck. Most nearby histories contain:

- Tess's death;
- severe hypoxic injury;
- survival with a different family member;
- major memory or personality discontinuity;
- a different guardian, education, and adult identity;
- later death or a dramatically different adaptation.

The adult Tess Evan meets is provisionally **more than one hundred effective bits rarer than a typical identity bundle**, with a working estimate in the 120-160 bit range. This is a fictional history-support measure, not an ordinary actuarial probability.

Rarity does not make her immune. It means the projector has almost no compliant neighboring versions to substitute. It must keep her recognizable or remove her entirely.

## Childhood and education

- Her grandparents are already dead at the time of the disaster.
- An aunt and cousins decline to take her in; Tess has little or no relationship with them afterward.
- Close family friends become her legal guardians and raise her in Fairbanks.
- She attends the University of Alaska Fairbanks.
- She moves to the Portland area after college.

## Ordinary life before Evan

Tess is a mildly eccentric amateur naturalist.

- Catalogs mushrooms, tide-pool organisms, rocks, weather, and birds she cannot reliably identify by sound.
- Owns field guides, binoculars, labeled specimens, small weather instruments, and abandoned attempts at watercolor.
- Has a cat whose name and personality remain open. The cat later moves into Evan and Ruth's house with her when the research becomes intensive.
- Plays pub trivia with Lena.
- Has a small, ordinary, inhabited life that contrasts with the mythic rarity of her survival.

## Relationship with water

Tess loves shorelines, tide pools, and open water.

She still rides ferries when necessary, but:

- remains on the exterior deck;
- notes exits, rafts, crew stations, and weather;
- keeps coat and boots on;
- does not sleep below the waterline.

The ocean is not the simple object of fear. Enclosure, failed machinery, and inaccessible exits are the deeper trigger.

## Relationship with Lena

Lena and Tess are genuine friends on the same pub-trivia league. Lena trusts her technical judgment and moral steadiness. Tess agrees to audit Evan partly as a professional challenge and partly as a favor to Lena.

## Relationship with Ruth

Tess develops an independent friendship with Ruth and spends time with her as she fades.

This relationship:

- gives Ruth companionship outside Evan's experiment;
- takes some of the sting out of Evan's distraction without excusing it;
- lets Tess witness Evan's love and failure at the same time;
- gives Tess a concrete reason to stay before she admits she is staying for Evan;
- grounds the love story in caregiving and shared domestic life rather than only cosmic compatibility.

## Relationship with Evan

First impression: Evan is probably a crazy person with unusually good instrumentation.

Over time, Tess finds his intensity, technical imagination, awkwardness, and obsessive curiosity endearing. He is the first person who describes her survival as a **statistical catastrophe with one living remainder**, not a miracle, destiny, proof of strength, or divine selection.

He gives her the possibility that survival can acquire chosen meaning without that meaning traveling backward to justify the deaths.

She understands why he is obsessed. She is also the person who insists that understanding does not excuse abandoning Ruth's remaining present for a hypothetical cure.

## Relationship rupture

Evan secretly uses Tess's uninformed choices as the live control in an experiment that tests her resistance to pruning. He withholds the experimental role because awareness would contaminate the result.

He has also used ferry, rescue, and medical records to estimate her causal rarity without fully involving her. The research is technically relevant, but he has converted her family's deaths and her survival into model parameters.

During the confrontation, Evan makes the deeper mistake: he argues that their meeting and love may have been inevitable within the surviving fixed-point history.

Tess hears him turning:

- her family's deaths;
- Ruth's decline;
- the erased universes;
- and his own violations

into proof that their relationship was supposed to happen.

The rupture does not require her to stop loving him. It requires Evan to understand that love does not confer retroactive permission.

## Why she stays

Her reasons develop in stages:

1. **Professional responsibility:** Lena needs an evaluator outside Evan's existing influence.
2. **Self-preservation:** Once Tess's own records begin contradicting one another, leaving does not remove her from the event. It only removes her capacity to observe or resist.
3. **Ruth:** Tess stays for the person who is being lost while Evan works to save her.
4. **Evan:** She loves him without endorsing what he does.
5. **Terminal necessity:** She refuses to let Evan become another sole survivor, sole witness, or sole author of reality.

## Epiphany

Tess begins with:

> Survival means nothing. Meaning is a story people attach afterward.

She ends with:

> Survival did not happen for a reason, but I can still choose what I do with it.

Chosen meaning does not redeem the disaster, excuse Evan, or turn the dead into necessary steps.

## Genesis role

High-level canon:

- Evan cannot create or repair the terminal state alone.
- Tess must remain a genuinely independent, nonparallel causal vector.
- Their final choices must preserve the real possibility of refusal and cannot be verified or coerced in advance.
- The act becomes genesis and does not preserve them as intact selves.
- The result creates chosen meaning without turning earlier deaths and violations into necessary or justified steps.

A mutual-veto assurance-game structure is the leading candidate because it directly resolves Tess's active false belief and reverses Evan's earlier use of her as an uninformed control. Exact terminal operations, payoff states, and physical control logic are deliberately deferred until the final chapter or two are outlined.

## Arc landmarks

- **Starting state:** Grounded, skeptical, and determined not to turn survival into destiny.
- **Entrance:** Lena brings her to audit Evan's causal coincidence chains.
- **First reversal:** She proves the global conjunction is anomalous even though each local link is ordinary.
- **Personal reversal:** Her own records begin contradicting one another while her identity remains stable.
- **Domestic deepening:** Tess and her cat move into Evan and Ruth's house; she becomes close to Ruth and falls in love with Evan.
- **Rupture:** Evan uses her unknowingly as a control and converts her survival history into experimental material.
- **Confrontation:** He calls their relationship inevitable; she rejects the retroactive justification.
- **Reconciliation:** Evan must give her every relevant fact and surrender the ability to verify her choice.
- **Ending:** Tess and Evan make genuinely independent terminal choices that neither can verify in advance. Their difference seeds genesis; the exact operation remains deliberately unplanned until late outlining.
