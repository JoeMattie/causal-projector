# Lena Varga

## Dramatic role

Lena is the skeptical scientific collaborator, not the terminal love interest.

## One-sentence description

A shared-facility experimental physicist who believes Evan's data before she
believes his interpretation and becomes the person most willing to stop a
fascinating experiment while its meaning is still uncertain.

## Work and discipline

Lena is a staff experimental physicist at a broad university shared research
facility. Her own field is experimental condensed matter, with a specific focus
on fundamental vortex phases and pinning in thin-film type-II superconductors.

She uses low-temperature equipment, transport measurements, field probes, and
other precision instruments as normal tools. She is not a specialist in bulk
melt-textured YBCO, materials characterization, superconducting-device design,
thermomagnetic flux pumps, MRI magnets, or adaptive control systems.

Her expertise is diagnostic rather than generative. She can recognize vortex
behavior that does not fit ordinary expectations and design a test that
separates explanations. She could not have told Evan how to build his garage
flux source.

## Motivation

Determine whether the anomaly is real and what it means while keeping
observation separate from desire. She wants to prevent elegance, usefulness,
personal loyalty, or her own curiosity from being mistaken for evidence of
safety.

## Immediate goal

Reproduce the anomaly under independent controls, identify its physical and informational boundary, and contain it before Evan expands the affected system.

## False beliefs

### Primary: faith in process

Lena believes an experiment can always be made safer by moving judgment out of
one person's hands and into an independent, auditable process.

This is a sound rule in ordinary science. It fails here because measurements,
approvals, stop criteria, human decisions, and automated controls can
collectively become a causally active carrier. Distributed responsibility does
not guarantee causal independence.

### Secondary: faith in evidence

Lena believes sufficiently clear evidence will compel reasonable people and
institutions to act responsibly.

The operation does not need anyone to reject the evidence. It can select
histories in which sincere, evidence-based responses compose into another live
closure.

## Personal stake

Lena has spent her career watching apparent anomalies disappear when the
controls improve. This one survives every attempt to kill it. It may be the
only genuinely new physical phenomenon she will ever encounter.

If she stops too early, she may spend the rest of her life knowing she
abandoned a discovery that could have changed physics. Her desire is not
primarily fame or ownership. She needs to know whether the effect is real and
what kind of reality could contain it.

Her growth requires accepting that a good question does not entitle her to an
answer. Choosing to leave the phenomenon genuinely unknown can be an ethical
act rather than a failure of science.

## Primary conflict

Lena's rigor makes the discovery credible, but every successful control also strengthens a phenomenon she increasingly believes should not be run. Evan values her skepticism until it produces a veto.

## Relationship with Evan

Before the garage anomaly, Evan commissioned or repaired the controls for an
unrelated instrument at Lena's shared facility. Lena was responsible for
acceptance. The controller met its written specification, but she withheld
signoff because its logs established controller state without proving that the
physical instrument had followed it.

They eventually found a mundane coupling outside both of their initial models.
Lena remembers Evan as technically excellent and too willing to treat a clean
control model as physical truth. Evan remembers her as the person who can hold
up a working system until the evidence earns the word `working`.

They become intellectual collaborators with genuine respect and escalating
moral conflict.

Evan calls Lena into the garage project only after the live consequential-bit
test convinces him that he has discovered new physics. He asks her to examine
the physical result because he trusts her to attack it. He does not report the
clarity as a suspected apparatus effect or recruit her to assess his mental
state.

Lena nevertheless identifies that connection first. She aligns Evan's manual
corrections and spoken notes with the sensor-rank collapse, asks him what he
experienced, and confirms the timing in a blinded repeat. She calls it an
operator effect without treating the correlation as proof of enhancement, mind
control, or a mechanism.

Lena does not oppose human exposure merely because an operator effect exists.
When Ruth experiences incidental lucidity during a causally live task and asks
for more, Lena initially supports reduced, monitored runs. Each interval gives
Ruth more continuity and lasts longer, with no incompatible detail in the
early results. The granddaughter memory appears during the clearest interval
yet and is what first makes Lena object to continuing. Her earlier controls are
cooperative safeguards, not an attempt to stop the work.

Evan calls the interval an overshoot and proposes optimizing for the strongest
lucid state whose autobiography agrees with his record family. Lena rejects
that as authentication. It can select a Ruth who conforms to Evan's history,
but it cannot establish that she is the original or correct Ruth. Evan turns
this objection into a reason for better controls and begins formalizing
another reduced run.

## Entity contact

Lena's first direct encounters with the entities are what ultimately cause her
to abandon the project. The experience makes it impossible for her to treat
the anomaly as only an unusual measurement problem.

Her contact remains preconceptual, extra-spatial, and resistant to reliable
symbolic reconstruction. Lena accepts it as evidence that the observer and the
containment process are causally coupled to the anomaly. She does not treat the
reconstructed entities, their apparent identities, or their cosmological lore
as authenticated fact.

The experience makes her reinterpret the passive evidence and recognize that
the containment system may no longer stand outside what it measures. Stronger
proof would require another live consequential response, which could strengthen
the carrier she is trying to establish.

## Break with Evan

Lena finds enough passive evidence to suspect that the distributed containment
process has become a causally active carrier. Proving it would require a live
consequential test whose result would feed directly into the system's next
response.

She refuses to run that test. Evan argues that suspicion will not convince
every participating lab and institution to disengage. Lena recognizes that
obtaining the evidence needed to justify stopping would itself strengthen the
thing they need to stop.

Evan pursues confirmation anyway. The test outcome determines the participating
labs' next containment or decommissioning action. Measurements, decisions,
approvals, and responses close a live projection cycle without Evan or the
original garage apparatus. Lena chooses ethical uncertainty, Evan still needs
the answer, and the result proves her suspected carrier by strengthening it.
Their scientific disagreement becomes a decisive rupture.

## Relationship with Tess

Friends and members of the same pub-trivia league. Lena brings Tess into the project because compound-risk analysis is outside her own expertise and because she trusts Tess to notice when a persuasive story has replaced a statistical argument.

Lena recruits Tess after the first precommitted rare physical outcome, when
longer coincidence chains begin appearing outside the immediate apparatus.
Tess arrives before Ruth's incidental lucidity. Her ferry history and causal
rarity play no role in the recruitment.

## Connection to the science

Lena designs the independent replication, separates live consequence from replay, insists on blinded controls, and helps formulate the finite-history model while marking the unsupported bridges. She should be the character most disciplined about what an experiment does **not** establish.

## Late-story role and end

After breaking with Evan, Lena remains an independent witness for as long as
coherent scientific observation is still possible. She will not initiate,
approve, or participate in another live feedback cycle. Any later work is
limited to passive analysis that cannot guide the system's next response. This
allows her to recognize the operation's self-exhaustion without undoing her
refusal or becoming a third terminal chooser.

During the several weeks of terminal brittleness, Evan and Tess try to reach
her. Tess seeks her friend. Evan chooses Lena as a person rather than her work,
data, or another explanation.

Lena has gone to a remote university geomagnetic observatory after leaving the
main shared facility implicated in the distributed carrier. She converts the
station to strictly receive-only operation. Passive magnetometry, independent
clocks, incoming data, and local storage let her observe without sending
measurements or conclusions into an institutional response or commanding any
replica site. This is an ethical boundary, not a safe haven, and she does not
believe isolation places her outside the operation. Tess knows the destination
from their last direct contact before communications fail.

Evan and Tess reach the observatory after the synchronized satellite collisions
and before the geologic catastrophe. Lena recognizes them, understands why she
came to the station, and can still share what passive observation has
established, but her continuity is beginning to thin. This is not dementia and
must not repeat Ruth's medical decline. It arises because the parent history
can no longer sustain new distinctions, consistent recent records, and
continuous personhood. The three experience the geologic escalation together:
synchronized continental-margin ruptures and
submarine landslides drive tsunamis and long-period waves that make the planet
ring. Evan reads their convergence in the station's plots and instruments;
Tess experiences their arrival as a planet-scale acoustic and bodily relation.
Evan does not convert Lena's decline into another diagnostic or rescue project.

Lena's continuity ends through fixed-successor collapse. She retains old
knowledge, recognizes Evan and Tess, and understands the onset, but different
new questions, observations, and choices increasingly lead to the same sentence
or action. Her past remains available while alternative next responses
disappear. While she can still make one last distinct choice, she refuses to
let Evan test her or turn the collapse into data. Evan honors the refusal and
stays with her until the person capable of making a different next choice is
gone.

By the approach to genesis, Lena no longer exists in any meaningful sense. She
is not killed for refusing, deliberately pruned as an opponent, converted into
a carrier, or preserved offstage. The parent universe is coming apart badly
enough that the continuity of people, institutions, records, and stable causal
separation can no longer be maintained. Lena disappears with that structure
before Evan and Tess reach the genesis boundary.

## Arc direction

- Begins by expecting interference, bad metrology, or operator contamination.
- Reproduces the anomaly independently.
- Helps identify residual entities and finite effective support.
- Experiences direct entity contact and can no longer treat the anomaly as
  only a measurement problem.
- Recognizes earlier than Evan that coherence is not authentication and that continued observation may itself cause damage.
- Attempts shutdown or containment.
- Brings Tess into the project.
- Refuses the live test needed to prove that the containment system is itself a
  carrier.
- Breaks with Evan when he pursues that confirmation anyway and establishes the
  first independent carrier.
- Accepts that refusing the next experiment may leave the central question
  permanently unanswered.
- Continues only as an independent, passive witness while coherent observation
  remains possible.
- Recognizes the operation's self-exhaustion without helping close another live
  cycle.
- Loses meaningful personal continuity as the parent universe comes apart,
  before Evan and Tess reach genesis.
