# Evan Hale

## One-sentence description

A workaholic controls engineer who responds to helplessness by solving problems, even when the work itself becomes the thing destroying what he hopes to save.

## Motivation

To protect Ruth from decline and prove that his care can accomplish something concrete.

## Immediate goal

Understand the garage anomaly and perfect it well enough to restore or stabilize Ruth's mind.

## Fatal flaw / false belief

Evan believes useful work is the highest form of love. When presence cannot fix suffering, he experiences presence as surrender and converts grief into engineering.

He also equates reduced ambiguity with truth. If one answer becomes clear, he assumes the alternatives were noise rather than part of reality's moral and cognitive load-bearing structure.

## Primary conflict

Every success pulls him away from Ruth, validates his worst coping mechanism, and removes versions of himself capable of stopping. He becomes increasingly correct by erasing worlds in which he is wrong.

## Relationship with Ruth

Ruth is his mother, assistant, intellectual companion, dependent, and moral witness. The garage project begins as a way to remain close. Evan's tragedy is that he eventually chooses the machine while telling himself he is choosing Ruth.

## Relationship with Tess

Tess initially treats him as a brilliant crank. Evan is drawn to the fact that she remains resistant, argumentative, and recognizably herself while everyone else converges. He recognizes the statistical violence of her survival without calling it destiny, then later violates that understanding by turning her rarity into an experimental control.

## Relationship with Lena

Lena is his skeptical collaborator and scientific conscience. She validates the phenomenon, sharpens the theory, and repeatedly insists on controls, consent, and shutdown. Evan values her rigor until it blocks the next experiment.

## Connection to the science

Evan is an approximate local eigenpattern because of his behavioral coherence, technical reach, and repeated willingness to continue. He initially thinks he is the dominant attractor. The deeper truth is that the projector operation uses him as a temporary biological bootloader.

## Essential backstory

1. Controls engineer and garage citizen scientist; the MRI flux project is plausible within his skills but depends on scavenged hardware, cross-domain reading, and stubborn instrumentation rather than universal genius.
2. Moves in with Ruth when she can no longer live alone.
3. A prior miscarriage exposed his habit of converting pain into work. He yelled while his wife grieved, helped bury the miscarriage in a planter, neglected the flowers, and contributed to the marriage's collapse by disappearing into labor.
4. Builds an air liquefier from a salvaged cellular-rack cryocooler.
5. Recognizes the machine's later residual geometry from prior DMT experience.

## Arc landmarks

- **Starting state:** Loving, competent, sad, and already overworking; the garage gives him and Ruth a shared language.
- **First reversal:** The anomaly produces real clarity and an objectively successful insight.
- **Escalation:** He proves the machine can force an improbable but physically valid outcome.
- **Moral injury:** Ruth becomes lucid but remembers a surviving granddaughter absent from his history.
- **Addiction:** Continued runs become euphoric and ontologically self-selecting.
- **Loss of control:** The distributed containment and replication system
  becomes an active carrier before he understands the full cosmological cost.
- **Recognition:** The entities are dying history families; the Quitter contains versions of him who stopped.
- **Failure:** He repeatedly treats people, consent, and resistance as variables in a better experiment.
- **Epiphany:** He has mistaken reduction for rescue throughout his life.
- **Separation from the operation:** He makes a sincere, non-instrumental
  refusal and chooses helpless presence over another attempt to run, fix, or
  control the operation. The independent carrier continues without him.
- **Ending:** He cannot create genesis alone. He must trust Tess's independent choice without modeling or controlling it.

## Signature texture

- Moves two fingers in a small clockwise circle while tracing control flow.
- Explains science at length as a form of intimacy.
- Labels and logs everything, then uses good documentation to make dangerous continuation feel responsible.
- Notices broken systems and unfinished repairs everywhere.
- Finds waiting almost physically intolerable.
